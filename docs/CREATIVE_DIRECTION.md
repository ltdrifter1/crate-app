# PlanetMP3 — Creative Direction

**YOUR WORLD. YOUR MUSIC.**

This is the single source of truth for PlanetMP3's creative vision, brand, and visual direction. All design and product decisions should be checked against this document.

---

## What PlanetMP3 is

PlanetMP3 is a music platform built around discovery, ownership, and collecting.

Instead of treating music as an endless stream of disposable content, PlanetMP3 makes the listener an active participant. You build your library. You make your playlists. You discover strange records. You follow charts. You request music. You collect favourites. You decide what matters.

It should feel less like a corporate streaming service and more like stepping into a digital record shop, radio station, MP3 player, and music community from an alternate 2002 — rebuilt with modern technology and glassmorphism.

**The feeling:** Y2K / early-internet optimism + underground music culture + modern technology. Not nostalgic for nostalgia's sake — the goal is to capture the feeling of when the internet made music feel new, weird, personal, and discoverable.

## Mission

PlanetMP3 exists to make music feel personal again.

We want to build a music platform where discovery is exciting, collections matter, and listeners have more control over how they experience music. PlanetMP3 brings together the best parts of the digital music era — MP3s, playlists, charts, radio, discovery, and community — and reimagines them for today.

## Creative vision

PlanetMP3 should feel like the internet's weird little record store.

A place where you can stumble across something you've never heard before, build a playlist for Friday night, see what people were listening to in 2004, discover a new artist, request a track, or spend an hour digging through music simply because it's fun.

We don't want to recreate Spotify. We want to recreate the feeling of discovering music.

The visual and cultural language should draw from early digital music culture: MP3 players, CD-Rs, message boards, music magazines, download stores, radio charts, bootleg flyers, rave culture, computer graphics, and Y2K-era web design.

But the product itself should be fast, clean, and contemporary. **Old internet soul. Modern music technology.**

## The 5 core principles

### 1. Discovery
Make finding music feel like digging through a record shop. Not everything should be algorithmically optimized for maximum engagement — there should be room for weirdness, obscurity, and surprise.

### 2. Ownership
The user's music collection should feel like *their* collection. Celebrate playlists, libraries, downloads, favourites, collections, and personal organization rather than treating everything as temporary streaming content.

### 3. Participation
Listeners shouldn't just consume. They can:
- Request music
- Build playlists
- Follow charts
- Create collections
- Recommend tracks
- Discover artists
- Participate in the music ecosystem

### 4. Music Culture
PlanetMP3 is about music culture, not just music playback — charts, history, editorial, underground scenes, artwork, liner-note culture, playlists, DJs, labels, and independent artists.

### 5. Fun
Probably the most important principle. The product should feel fun to explore. Little details should reward curiosity.

## The 5 product goals

**Goal 01 — Build the best personal music experience**
Make PlanetMP3 an excellent place to organize, play, and interact with music.

**Goal 02 — Make discovery addictive**

**Goal 03 — Make music history accessible**
Explore what was happening in different years, genres, and scenes. This turns PlanetMP3 into something closer to a music archive + discovery engine rather than another streaming catalogue.

**Goal 04 — Build a community around music**
Make people feel like they're inside a music scene, not simply using an app.

**Goal 05 — Make the interface iconic**
PlanetMP3 should be recognizable without the logo. Think: Y2K digital music culture × underground club flyer × futuristic MP3 player. Not overly polished corporate SaaS. Not generic Spotify black. Not fake-retro skeuomorphism. Instead: digital, playful, slightly strange, collectible, energetic.

## Creative direction

**Y2K digital culture × underground music culture × modern technology.**

The aesthetic should feel:
- Digital
- Playful
- Underground
- Futuristic
- Nostalgic without being retro
- Slightly weird
- Music-first

Think: MP3 player + record shop + music magazine + club flyer + early internet.

### Product philosophy
PlanetMP3 should never feel like another generic streaming app. It should feel like a place you want to explore. Every screen should encourage curiosity, discovery, and personal connection with music.

### The important rule
Don't make everything retro. PlanetMP3 should not look like a fake Windows 95 website.

It should look like someone in 2003 imagined what the future of music would look like — and they were right.

### The formula
- 40% modern product design
- 25% Y2K digital culture
- 20% underground music culture
- 15% weird PlanetMP3 personality

The app should ultimately feel like: **a music machine from another planet.**
